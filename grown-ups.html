For parents, carers and grown-ups

Zibbitz is a game on Roblox. It isn't open to players yet: it runs on a test copy while it is built. This page says what it is meant to be, what it already does, and how to keep a child safe on Roblox.

Why Zibbitz exists

Many people, young and old, are growing up into a world where it is hard to know what they are for. Zibbitz is a place to practise becoming yourself: it asks nothing of a player but to be who they are, and it is measured by what people carry out of it, not by how long they stay.

There are no classes to pick. A character grows from what the player keeps choosing to do: making things, helping, exploring, fighting monsters. The game tries to show them who that is, without telling them who to be.

Good for children, and for the grown-ups beside them

Zibbitz is built to be better with someone beside you, and never worse:

  • Players can heal and shield each other, and some pets mend friends nearby.
  • Two people resting on the same seat both rest better.
  • Seeds planted in the town garden grow for whoever comes by, and anyone may pick them when they are ripe.
  • Broken benches and fallen stones by the roads are mended by many people together, one gift each, and stay mended for everyone.
  • Some big creatures are calmed by everyone in the world together, and a stone remembers it afterwards with nobody's name on it.

Every player chooses who may do what to them (heal and shield them, play tricks on them, trade and teach, push or carry them): anyone, friends only, ask me first, or nobody. A child can play beside a grown-up, or alone, and lose nothing either way. Players don't fight each other.

The game's stories are written to be read twice: a child gets the fun and one clear lesson, and the grown-up next to them may catch a second layer to talk about afterwards. Monsters are never evil; they are stuck, and can be helped.

Patterns we avoid

We checked each of these against the game as it is built today, and list only what holds:

  • Nothing is sold for Robux. The game has no shop for real money at all today. If anything is ever sold, it will be for looks only, priced up front, and never a random item or anything that makes a character stronger.
  • No daily login rewards, streaks or countdowns. There is no reward for coming back every day and nothing to lose by missing one.
  • Time away costs nothing. Pets don't get hungry or sad while a player is away, and nothing a player owns shrinks or decays.
  • No nagging. No guilt messages when a player leaves, and no notifications sent outside the game.
  • No rewards for inviting friends.
  • No scoreboards ranking players by level, wealth or time played. A rank shows how a character has grown, not that one player is better than another.
  • No ads.
  • Bad luck always ends. Where the game uses chance (like improving a piece of gear), a run of misses is always followed by a success, and a miss never breaks anything.

Some things in the game are random, like what a monster leaves behind. Chance is never sold.

Safety and parental controls

Most of the controls that keep a child safe belong to Roblox, not to us, and they work in every game. If your child plays on Roblox, these are worth setting up:

  • Link your account to your child's. A parent account on Roblox can manage a child's settings from one place.
  • Spending. You can set a monthly spending limit and be told when your child tries to buy something. Zibbitz sells nothing today, but other games on Roblox do.
  • Chat. Roblox asks every player to complete an age check before they can chat, and then lets them chat only with people in similar age groups. For children under nine, chat in games is off unless a parent agrees. You can also choose who your child may chat with. Every word one player sends another in Zibbitz goes through Roblox's own filter, and the game has no private channel that goes around these settings.
  • Time. You can set a daily time limit, and see how long your child played each day of the week.
  • Content. You can choose which content maturity your child may see, and block games.
  • Reporting. If someone behaves badly, use Roblox's Report button. In Zibbitz, a ladybird button also lets a child tell us when something looks broken, by tapping a picture, with no typing.

You will find these in the Roblox app's settings, under Parental Controls. Roblox explains the chat age checks here.

A word on time: Zibbitz is made so that stopping is always fine. Nothing is lost by putting it down, and a good session can be short. If your child plays for a while, ask them what they made, who they helped, or what they found. Those are the parts we hope they carry out with them.

The game never asks a player who they are outside it, and never leads anyone off Roblox.

Privacy in plain English

  • This site sets no cookies, runs no analytics and collects nothing. Its host, GitHub, logs each visitor's internet address for security, as it does for every site it hosts (Privacy says more).
  • The game keeps what it needs to remember a player's character: their level, their things, their pets, what they built. All of it can be read by the player in the game, in the Diary in their Journal.
  • Nothing about one player leaves the game. What leaves are totals of many players together (for example, how smoothly the game runs on phones), with no names or ids, so nobody can be picked out.
  • A broken-thing report from the ladybird keeps where it happened in the game, the kind of device, and which errors the game saw, with no name or id.
  • The game never asks for a real name, age, school, address or anything else about who a player is outside it.
  • To ask for your child's data to be deleted, use Roblox's own data privacy request (from Roblox's Support page). Roblox passes these requests on to the games that player played, and Zibbitz has a tool that removes the player's save and what the world keeps under their name.
