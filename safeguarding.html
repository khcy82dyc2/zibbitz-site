Safeguarding

Children play on Roblox, and Zibbitz is built for them to be safe in it. This page says how, and what to do if you are worried about a child.

If a child is in danger now

  • Call your local emergency number. We are a very small team and can't respond in an emergency.
  • In the UK, if a child is being contacted or pressured online in a sexual way, you can report it to CEOP, part of the National Crime Agency.
  • To report a player, use Roblox's Report button, in the game's menu or on the player. Roblox reviews reports and can act on accounts; we can't.

How the game is built

  • No private channels. The game has no chat or messages of its own that go around Roblox's settings.
  • Very few words between players. The only words one player can write for others are on their own home: a short banner, guestbook lines and notes. Every one goes through Roblox's text filter before anyone else sees it, guestbook lines and notes are filtered again when they are read, and if the filter can't be reached the words are not shown at all. They are shown only to players whose Roblox settings let them chat.
  • No pictures, sounds or links can be sent from one player to another.
  • Nothing about life outside. The game never asks a player who they are outside it, never links off Roblox, and doesn't read where in the world a player is.
  • No romance. Characters are friends, family, neighbours and teammates. The game has nothing for romance between players.
  • Players don't fight each other, and each player chooses who may do what to them (heal and shield them, play tricks on them, push or carry them, swap things or teach): anyone, friends only, ask me first, or nobody.
  • Everyone is treated as a child. Zibbitz does not know how old a player is, so every player gets the protections meant for children.
  • Roblox's parental controls (chat, spending, time, content) apply in full inside Zibbitz, and the game adds no way around them. The grown-ups page explains them.

Telling us

  • A child can tell us something in the game looks broken with the ladybird button, by tapping pictures. It carries no name and no typed words, so it can't be used to reach anyone.
  • A grown-up can write to us through the contact form once it is open. A message that suggests a child may be in danger is passed to the creator at once, marked urgent.
  • Nobody working on Zibbitz contacts players directly, and we never write to a child.
