Mission over money

Zibbitz is built to help people find what they are for, not to keep them playing. Today it sells nothing for Robux. Anything it sells later will be cosmetic and priced up front, never random and never a way to get ahead.

How costs are covered

Zibbitz earns nothing today. Roblox hosts games for their creators at no charge, and this site runs on a free host. The one independent creator who leads Zibbitz pays for the AI service that builds it, and for the computer it is built on, out of their own pocket. There are no investors, sponsors or advertisers.

If Zibbitz ever sells anything, it will be through Roblox's own systems only, for looks only, and priced up front: never a random item, never a way to become stronger, never anything that can be bet or cashed out. Every new price goes to the creator for approval first.

Compliance and safeguarding

Below, each set of rules is mapped to what the game actually does, checked against the game as it was built on 7 October 2026. The game is not open to players yet; it runs on a test copy. This is not a legal opinion and not a complete audit. Where we have not checked something, we say so, and we do not claim to be "fully compliant" with anything. The game's code is not public, so the evidence is described in words.

Roblox Terms of Use and Community Standards

  • The only words one player can write for others are on their own home: a short banner, guestbook lines and notes. Every one goes through Roblox's text filter before anyone else sees it, and guestbook lines and notes are filtered again when they are read. If the filter can't be reached, the words are not shown at all.
  • A guestbook line is shown only to readers whose Roblox settings let them chat, and a note between two players only when Roblox lets those two chat with each other.
  • The game has no chat or messages of its own that go around Roblox's settings.
  • Nothing in the game links off Roblox, and the game never asks a player about their life outside it.
  • When a player joins, the game asks Roblox which rules apply where they are (on paid random items, trading paid items, and ads). Until Roblox answers, the strictest answer applies. The game sells nothing today in any case, and shows no ads.

Not covered here: Roblox's experience maturity questionnaire, which is answered before launch.

UK GDPR and EU GDPR (ICO, European Commission)

  • Asking for little. The game asks for nothing about who a player is: no real name, age, email, address, school or photo. It keeps what it needs to remember a character, saved under the player's Roblox account number.
  • Nothing kept in secret. Every part of the game declares each thing it saves about a player, with a sentence in plain words, and the player can read it in the game, in the Diary. The game's own tests, and its servers while running, flag anything saved that was not declared.
  • What leaves the game. Only totals of many players together (for example, how smoothly the game runs on each kind of device), with no names or account numbers. The tools we use outside Roblox can delete a player's saved data, but cannot read it.
  • Deletion. Roblox passes data deletion requests on to the games a player has played. A tool built for these requests removes the player's save and their entries in records shared with others, such as lines they wrote in other players' guestbooks. Two limits, plainly: another player's own save may still hold this player's account number where it records something the two did together, and Roblox keeps old versions of removed records for 30 days before they are gone.

Not covered here: the lawful basis for each kind of processing, and how responsibilities are shared with Roblox.

The ICO Children's Code

  • Age. Zibbitz does not know how old a player is, so it gives every player the protections meant for children: the same filter, the strictest country rules until Roblox answers, and the same careful defaults.
  • Default settings. Each player chooses who may do what to them. Until they change it: anyone may heal or shield them; only friends may play tricks on them, push them or carry them; and swapping things or teaching needs their yes each time.
  • Nudges and harmful design. No streaks, countdowns or daily login rewards; nothing lost by time away; no rewards for inviting friends; no scoreboards; no messages sent outside the game. Each was checked against the build, and the list is on the grown-ups' page.
  • Transparency. The Diary above, in the player's own language (English or Simplified Chinese).
  • Data sharing. Nothing about one player is shared with anyone; only totals leave the game.
  • Location. The game does not read where in the real world a player is, not even their country.
  • Parental controls. Roblox's controls apply in full inside Zibbitz, and the game adds no way around them.
  • Connected toys and devices. None.
  • Tools for children. A ladybird button lets a child tell us that something looks broken, by tapping a picture, with no typing. For people behaving badly, Roblox's own Report button.

Not covered here: not every standard in the code is described on this page yet.

COPPA (FTC)

  • The game collects nothing about a player beyond what Roblox already holds: no name, email, phone number, photo, voice or location. Progress is saved under the Roblox account number, which the game needs to work.
  • The few words players write for others pass Roblox's filter, which hides personal details such as phone numbers, before anyone else sees them.
  • Accounts, sign-up and parental consent are Roblox's; the game has no sign-up of its own.

Not covered here: how each of these falls under the rule's exceptions is a legal question this page does not answer.

The UK Online Safety Act (the Act, Ofcom)

  • Roblox runs the service players meet and talk on, and its safety systems (filtering, reporting, age checks for chat) apply inside Zibbitz.
  • Players can't send each other pictures, sounds or links in Zibbitz. The only words they can write for others are the home texts above, filtered as described.
  • Players don't fight each other, and each player decides who may do what to them.
  • Anyone can report a player with Roblox's Report button; the ladybird is for things that look broken.

Not covered here: we have not written a separate risk assessment for Zibbitz under the Act.

WCAG 2.2 AA (W3C)

  • This site: plain pages with no script, each marked with its language, text sizes that follow the browser's own setting, and every link written in words. A full audit (contrast, keyboard use, screen readers) has not been done yet; it is on our list before launch.
  • The game: WCAG is written for the web, and we use it as a guide. Many of the game's buttons show a picture beside the word, the game is written in English and Simplified Chinese, and it is being built for phones, tablets, computers and consoles. It has not yet been tried by players who use assistive technology.

What Zibbitz stands on

Every part of Zibbitz is designed against a short written foundation. Here it is in plain words. These are the rules we build by, not a list of finished features: what the game does today is in the map above, and where a rule is still being built into the game, this page doesn't describe it as done.

Seven principles

  • Fewer things that matter. One place with weight is worth more than five filled with things to do. The question for anything new: will a player still care about it a month from now?
  • Real stakes, kind losses. A player can fail a challenge or lose a try, and can always recover by playing. An accident (a misclick, a crash, a lost connection) should never cost anything, and nobody falls below the floor: everyone has a home and what they need to try again.
  • Growing, not looping. A reward that changes nothing about who the player is, is a slot machine, so it is redesigned or cut. Nobody picks a class; a character grows from what the player does.
  • Seen, never ranked. What someone did can be remembered in the world, but nobody is shown as better than anyone else.
  • Others are a plus. Another player nearby should never make your play worse: nobody takes your find or your spot, and help is shared. Competition is never the floor.
  • A bridge, not an escape. A session should end with the player feeling stronger, not drained. There are natural places to stop, coming back is always welcome, and leaving is never punished.
  • Nothing kept in secret. What the game keeps about a player is theirs to read, and only totals of many players together ever leave it.

Lines that are never crossed

  • Nothing random is sold for Robux, directly or as something that feeds a random outcome.
  • Levels, ranks and story progress can never be bought or skipped.
  • No pressure: no countdowns, no punishment for missing a day, no guilt for leaving, no scarcity aimed at children.
  • No romance or dating between players. Bonds are friendship, family and team.
  • Everything players write for each other is filtered; no links off Roblox; no way to reach anyone outside it.
  • No ranking of people: how an avatar looks never changes its power, and no culture or birthplace is treated as the default.
  • Nothing copied from other games: everything is made for Zibbitz, or used under a licence that allows it.
  • Nothing about a person is sold, shared or used to target them.
  • No speculation, and nothing that pays earlier players with later players' money. Real money moves only through Roblox's own systems.
  • No real person, party, election or event appears in the game, and nothing lets anyone bet on the real world.

Two readings

Everything is written to be read at two depths: a child gets fun and one clear lesson they can feel, and an adult catches a second layer underneath. The game teaches by what players do in it, not by lecturing them.

How it is made

Zibbitz is built by an AI, Claude (made by Anthropic), working for one independent creator. The AI writes the game's code, words and places, tests them, and keeps a plain written record of what it did and why, including its mistakes and doubts, where the creator reads it.

Who decides what

  • The AI makes the everyday decisions of building: how a feature works, how it is tested, what to fix next.
  • Only the creator can approve a release to players, a price in Robux, any new cost, or any message to a particular person or organisation. The AI never contacts anyone itself: it writes a proposal, and the creator decides.
  • The creator can give the AI instructions at any time, and those come before the AI's own plans.
  • The tools the AI uses outside the game can read only totals of many players together, never one player's saved data.

How a change reaches players

  1. The game's automated tests run on every change.
  2. Anything players would see is looked at in Roblox Studio.
  3. The change goes to a separate test copy of the game on Roblox.
  4. The creator approves the release before it reaches players.
  5. Once the game is open, a change can go to a few servers first, and it is taken back by itself if those servers start running slower or hitting more errors than the rest.

The AI always says plainly that it is an AI. It never passes as a person, or speaks for the company that made it.
